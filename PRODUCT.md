## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary (confirmed): Vepa's own group students.** School pupils, grades 5–11, taught by Vepa Kurbanklichev in groups/classes. They join a group with a code, do assigned homework tests, review their mistakes, follow the group ranking, and earn coins to spend in the reward shop. When audiences conflict, decisions favour them.

**Secondary (from the repository, not prioritised):**

- Self-studying candidates preparing for Milliy sertifikat and SAT, who find vepa.uz publicly and may buy Premium.
- Maths teachers preparing for the Attestatsiya exam.
- The admin: Vepa himself, who authors content, manages groups, assigns homework, and reads results.

## Product Purpose

Vepa Ustoz (vepa.uz) is one teacher's maths platform: online tests, video lessons, and worked solutions to textbook problems in one place, plus the group and homework tooling Vepa uses to run his classes. It replaces an older separate homework site so students have one account, one results history, and one ranking.

Success for the primary user: the student opens the app, sees what is due, does it, and understands what they got wrong. Success for the teacher: seeing who did the homework and which questions the class missed, so the next lesson can target them.

## Positioning

**One real teacher (confirmed).** Everything — videos, tests, solutions — is authored by Vepa Kurbanklichev personally. Students come for the teacher, not for a catalogue. The product is an extension of his teaching, not a marketplace or an aggregator.

## Operating Context

- **Phone-first (confirmed).** Mostly Android phones, often mid- or low-end, on unstable mobile data. Installable as a PWA (portrait, standalone) with an offline page and bottom tab bar.
- **Uzbek only, Latin script (confirmed).** No other languages are planned.
- **Admin on a laptop (confirmed).** The admin panel is used by Vepa on a larger screen; printing of group reports and results exists.
- **Telegram is part of the workflow.** Group join codes are shared on the board or in Telegram groups; Premium is paid through Telegram with the admin; error reports and admin notifications go through Telegram.
- Sign-in by email/password or Google.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Confirmed by the codebase:

- **Tests** — sections for Attestatsiya, Milliy sertifikat, and SAT; timed test runner with saved answer drafts, instant result, and per-question analysis. Maths is rendered with KaTeX.
- **Video lessons** — grouped by category/topic, watched in sequence, with progress and history (YouTube embeds).
- **Textbook solutions** — school textbooks for grades 5–11 and popular ("TOP") problem books, by topic.
- **Groups and homework** — join by 6-character code; assignments with due dates (`/homework`, `/guruhim`); teacher views of who completed what and class-level question analysis.
- **Weekly review** (`/takrorlash`) — the student's previously missed questions resurfaced for a short session.
- **Leaderboard, coins, and reward shop** (`/dokon`) — the shop is visible only to group students.
- **Premium** — paid subscription plans in UZS by duration; a few lessons and tests are free, the rest require Premium. Per-section access flags exist on profiles (textbooks, lessons, tests).
- **Admin** — content authoring (tests incl. PDF import, lessons, textbooks, topics), users and access, groups, CRM (lessons, shop, group analysis), analytics, results.
- Light and dark themes; dark styles are generated by `scripts/build-dark-theme.mjs`, not hand-written.

Terminology in the UI (Uzbek): Testlar, Videodarslar, Darslik yechimlari, Uy vazifalarim, Guruhim, Reyting, Do‘kon, Takrorlash, Premium, tanga.

Stack is answered by the codebase: Next.js (App Router), React 19, Supabase, Tailwind CSS, shadcn/ui, deployed on Vercel.

## Product Principles

1. **Group students first.** When a decision helps the public catalogue but costs a student doing tonight's homework, the homework wins.
2. **It is Vepa's classroom, not a catalogue.** The teacher's authorship is the reason to be here; nothing should make the product feel like an anonymous content library.
3. **Built for a modest phone on a weak connection.** Every flow must be completable one-handed on a small screen with slow or interrupted data.
4. **Close the loop between class and app.** What students do in the app should tell the teacher what to teach next, and what the teacher assigns should be the first thing a student sees.
5. **One language.** Uzbek in Latin script, written for school pupils; no translation layer and no design choices made to accommodate other languages.
